# geodecider-lithology-agentic-workflow

**Covers:** A machine-learning/AI research paper (with appendices) presenting GeoDecider, an LLM-agent framework that combines a fast point-wise classifier with difficulty-aware routing and multi-perspective, tool-assisted reasoning for depth-wise lithology/facies classification from well logs.

**Authoritative for:** GeoDecider's architecture: two-stage fast classifier plus LLM agent refinement, difficulty scoring from classifier probabilities, and routing thresholds, Evidence Profile construction and the tool set (analog/neighbor retrieval, depth-wise trend analysis, prediction history, knowledge base) used by the agent, Benchmark results and ablations on FORCE 2020, Kansas Council Grove (Kaggle facies), SEAM, and GeoLink datasets versus XGBoost, GBDT, LSTMFCN and foundation-model baselines, The Flying Point Ratio (FPR) metric for isolated/spurious depth-point label predictions and how geological refinement reduces it, Dataset specifications: log curve input channels, class counts, well/sample counts, sampling intervals, train/test splits and leakage control, LLM backbone and temperature sensitivity ablations (DeepSeek-R1, GPT-5, Gemini 3 Pro), Prompt design for multi-agent lithofacies reasoning and a worked Wackestone vs. Packstone-grainstone case study

**Not good for:** Petrophysical calculation workflows (Vshale, porosity, saturation, cutoffs) or log quality-control and environmental corrections, Regional stratigraphy, depositional-system interpretation, or basin history for the North Sea, Kansas, or any study area, Core description, thin-section, or biostratigraphic lithofacies definition standards, Seismic interpretation, reservoir modeling, or volumetrics, Tool physics and log acquisition principles, Production-grade guidance on deploying LLMs in operational well-site or drilling decisions

**Scope:** Contemporary (LLM-era) computational geoscience method paper; evaluation is confined to four public labeled well-log benchmarks — FORCE 2020 (Norwegian North Sea, 5 lithologies), Kansas Council Grove Kaggle facies, SEAM (synthetic), and GeoLink — so accuracy figures, class schemes, and curve availability are dataset-specific and may not transfer to other basins, logging suites, or proprietary facies taxonomies. Results depend on specific LLM backbones available at time of writing.
