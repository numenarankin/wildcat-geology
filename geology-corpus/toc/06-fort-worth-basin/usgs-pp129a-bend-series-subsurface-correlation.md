# usgs-pp129a-bend-series-subsurface-correlation

**Covers:** A 1920s-era USGS Professional Paper section applying percentage/graphic logs of well cuttings to subdivide and correlate the Pennsylvanian "Bend series" between the Seaman and Rudd wells in the Fort Worth Basin, north-central Texas.

**Authoritative for:** construction and interpretation of "percentage logs" (sand-clay-lime proportions) from well cuttings, lettered unit subdivision (A through L) of the "Bend series" in the Seaman and Rudd wells and thickness ratios between them, use of autochthonous glauconite and phosphatic basal beds as markers of stratigraphic breaks/formation contacts, microscopic and hand-sample criteria for distinguishing black shale from black limestone, and caved vs in-place sand, in cuttings, early correlation of Rudd/Seaman units with the Smithwick, Marble Falls limestone, "Lower Bend" shale, and Plummer's "Millsap division", historical accounts of reported oil sands (Ranger sand and others) and their positions in the Bend section

**Not good for:** modern petrophysical or wireline log analysis, porosity/permeability quantification, or reserve estimates, current formal stratigraphic nomenclature — "Bend series" and many unit names here are obsolete or redefined, biostratigraphic or absolute age dating, basins outside north-central Texas, or Barnett Shale unconventional resource questions, regional structural, thermal-maturity, or source-rock geochemistry interpretation, core-based sedimentology or sequence stratigraphy

**Scope:** Early 20th-century USGS subsurface work limited to well-cuttings (sample) study of two principal wells plus scattered Ranger/Eastland-area logs in the Fort Worth Basin, north-central Texas; conclusions are lithologic-correlation only, with pre-modern terminology, arbitrary shale/limestone boundaries, and driller's-log-quality depth control.

_15 segments._
