# hardage-3d-seismic-carbonate-karst-collapse

**Covers:** A case-study chapter (Hardage) on using 3-D seismic to image Ellenburger karst-collapse chimneys and their compartmentalization of overlying Bend Conglomerate/Caddo gas reservoirs at Boonsville field, Fort Worth Basin, Texas, including the survey's acquisition, statics/velocity processing, and interpretation pitfalls.

**Authoritative for:** appearance of karst collapse chimneys on 3-D seismic time-structure and amplitude maps, size, spacing, and vertical extent of Ellenburger-rooted collapse features at Boonsville, karst-collapse mechanism and structural sag magnitude above collapsed caverns, compartmentalization of Bend Conglomerate/Caddo reservoirs by deep karst collapse (Sealy C-2/C-3 example), distinguishing buried statics artifacts from genuine collapse chimneys, Boonsville 3-D acquisition parameters, explosive source/shot-hole design, processing sequence, bin size, statics and velocity-analysis practice for high-frequency (>100 Hz) data

**Not good for:** carbonate reservoir petrophysics or log-based porosity/saturation calculations, Ellenburger regional stratigraphy, biostratigraphy, or depositional history, non-karst structural or tectonic trap interpretation, modern seismic workflows (pre-stack depth migration, full-waveform inversion, spectral decomposition, machine learning), unconventional Barnett Shale resource assessment or hydraulic fracturing, karst systems outside the Lower Paleozoic Midcontinent setting, quantitative reserves or economic evaluation

**Scope:** Single-field case study at Boonsville, Fort Worth Basin, north-central Texas; Ordovician Ellenburger karst beneath Pennsylvanian Bend Conglomerate/Caddo clastics at roughly 4,000-6,000 ft depths. Derives from mid-1990s DOE/GRI Secondary Gas Recovery work, so acquisition and processing practice reflect 1990s technology; conclusions are conventional gas-reservoir oriented and predate Barnett Shale development.
