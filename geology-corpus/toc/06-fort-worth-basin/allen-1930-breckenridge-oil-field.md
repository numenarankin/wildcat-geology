# allen-1930-breckenridge-oil-field

**Covers:** A 1930 University of Texas-era thesis describing the Breckenridge oil field, Stephens County, Texas: its geography, discovery and boom history, Bend/Strawn/Ellenberger stratigraphy, dome structure, crude properties, production statistics through 1928, and contemporary cable-tool drilling and nitroglycerine well-shooting practice.

**Authoritative for:** Breckenridge field discovery history, discovery wells, initial production rates, and boom chronology (1917-1928), the 1921 G.W. Keathly No.1 gas blowout and its oil, gas, and salt water rates, driller's terminology for the Bend section: 'Caddo lime', 'Breckenridge lime', 'False black lime', and distinguishing Smithwick from Strawn shale in cuttings, local Bend Group and Strawn Group thicknesses, pay horizon, and Ellenberger position at Breckenridge, Stephens County annual well completion and production counts, peak production and decline through 1928, Breckenridge crude gravity and composition; reported geothermal gradient at Breckenridge and Ranger, historical cable-tool drilling, casing records, well costs, and nitroglycerine shooting procedure (squib types, casing pull-back, spontaneous detonation in the Bend)

**Not good for:** modern petrophysics, log analysis, or reserve estimation methods, post-1930 production, secondary recovery, waterflood, or unconventional Barnett Shale development, modern sequence-stratigraphic or biostratigraphic nomenclature (terms are driller's and 1920s usage), regional Fort Worth Basin or Bend arch synthesis beyond the immediate Breckenridge vicinity, source-rock geochemistry, maturity, or migration modeling (source discussion is inferential and dated), any basin outside north-central Texas; current drilling, completion, or safety practice

**Scope:** Single-field study, Breckenridge/Stephens County, Texas, on the eastern Bend arch flank of the Fort Worth Basin; data end ca. 1928, written 1930. Structural interpretation predates seismic and modern well-log correlation; stratigraphic names are 1920s driller's and early-20th-century usage. Treat production figures and drilling economics as historical record, not current.

_10 segments._
