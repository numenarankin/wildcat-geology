# tearpock-bischke-applied-subsurface-geological-mapping

**Covers:** Section 06 of Tearpock & Bischke's *Applied Subsurface Geological Mapping* — the foundational chapters on contouring, computer surface modeling, directional/horizontal well geometry, electric log correlation, and integration of 2D/3D seismic into subsurface maps.

**Authoritative for:** rules, methods, and pitfalls of hand contouring (mechanical, parallel, equal-spaced, interpretive) and choice of contour interval, computer contouring theory: gridding vs. triangulation, search algorithms (sector/octant), why gridded maps fail to honor data points, conformable/stacking mapping to build deep-horizon structure maps from shallow maps plus isochore maps, contouring faulted surfaces: trace faults, fault polygons, restored-surface method, directional and horizontal well terminology (KOP, build rate, L/S-shape, long/medium/short radius), well plan design, and survey calculation methods (radius of curvature vs. minimum curvature), directional survey uncertainty and reconciling structural depths to a common fluid contact, thickness conversions among MLT, TVDT, TVT, TST, TSD, including the 3D TVT equation and worked examples, electric log correlation procedure: type logs, correlation plans, shale resistivity markers, closed-loop correlation, distinguishing faults from stratigraphic thinning, bed-dip effects, unconformities, and repeated sections on logs, estimating missing section and restored tops in vertical and deviated wells, on-screen/computer log correlation workflow, log gapping, datum choice, and log annotation conventions, principles of integrating seismic with well control: loop tying, phantoming, sideswipe, synthetic seismograms and VSP well ties, 2D vs. 3D imaging limits

**Not good for:** basin-specific stratigraphy, play descriptions, or Fort Worth Basin / Barnett Shale geology despite the section filename, petrophysical calculations: porosity, saturation, net-pay cutoffs, log response modeling, seismic acquisition and processing parameters, migration algorithms, or AVO/inversion, structural balancing details, fault-surface and Allan-diagram construction, salt or thrust-belt mapping specifics (later chapters of the book), reserve estimation, volumetrics, economics, or drilling engineering practice, modern automated/AI seismic interpretation and machine-learning gridding tools

**Scope:** Petroleum-industry subsurface mapping textbook, US Gulf Coast/Gulf of Mexico extensional-terrane bias in examples; workflows are technique- and geometry-based, not region- or age-specific, so equations (TVT, TST, TSD, restored tops) transfer anywhere. Software discussion reflects late-1990s/2000s workstation practice; the general gridding-vs-triangulation cautions still hold but named tools and screen workflows are dated. Assumes well-log and 2D/3D seismic data; no coverage of outcrop or non-hydrocarbon mapping.
