# medcrave-elsharafi-perforation-skin-hsd-guns-print

**Covers:** A short journal paper (MedCrave) applying Karakas–Tariq perforation-skin theory and Hawkins damage-skin equations to estimate composite skin and pre-perforation flow rate for HSD perforating guns in four vertical MOL Hungary oil wells.

**Authoritative for:** step-by-step Karakas & Tariq perforation skin procedure (plane-flow Sb, vertical converging Sv, wellbore Swb) with phasing coefficient tables, Hawkins / Craft & Hawkins damage skin from altered-zone permeability and radius, and back-calculating ka from total skin, enumerating components of total skin (damage, completion, perforation, crushed/compacted zone, gravel pack, partial penetration, geometry), estimating perforation penetration depth from formation effective compressive strength (overburden gradient minus reservoir pressure), worked numeric examples of composite skin and steady-state oil flow rate for 60° phasing, 6 spf HSD guns with 19.2–26 in penetration, qualitative ranking of HSD gun types by resulting flow rate and sensitivity of flow rate to perforation depth, shot density and phasing

**Not good for:** horizontal, deviated, or fractured/multi-stage completions, gun hardware engineering, charge design, explosive loading, or API RP 19B test procedures, underbalanced/dynamic-underbalance perforating physics and transient cleanup modeling, reservoir characterization, geology, or stratigraphy of the Hungarian fields (input data only, no geologic interpretation), pressure-transient analysis or field-measured skin validation (skins are computed, not well-test derived), gravel-pack, frac-pack, or sand-control skin design despite gravel-pack skin being listed, modern coupled numerical/CFD perforation flow simulation

**Scope:** Analytical/spreadsheet study of four vertical oil wells operated by MOL in Hungary; assumes single-phase steady-state radial flow, homogeneous formation, and assumed values for damaged-zone radius, crushed-zone permeability and kv/kh where data are missing. Correlations (Standing–Harris, Karakas–Tariq, Hawkins) are 1970s–1990s vintage and applied outside their original phasing/geometry ranges in places. Results are predictive estimates without field production validation; treat numbers as illustrative rather than calibrated. Predatory-adjacent open-access venue, so peer review depth is uncertain.
