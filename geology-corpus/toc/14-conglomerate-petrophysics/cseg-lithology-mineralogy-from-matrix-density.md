# cseg-lithology-mineralogy-from-matrix-density

**Covers:** A CSEG-style petrophysics paper deriving lithology and mineralogy from matrix density using wireline density/neutron/gamma-ray logs, worked through the Glauconitic Sandstone at Blackfoot field, Alberta.

**Authoritative for:** density-log physics: dual-detector design, electron density index, and the rho_log = 1.0704*rho_e - 0.188 conversion, 2(Z/A) values for common elements (H, Si, Ca, etc.) and why the density log departs from true bulk density in water-filled porosity, tabulated bulk and electron densities for quartz, calcite, dolomite, pyrite, siderite, anhydrite, coal and shale, workflow for back-calculating matrix density from logs when no core is available, and sensitivity of computed porosity to matrix-density error, reported matrix-density values for the Glauconitic Sandstone in three Blackfoot wells, and effect of assumed fluid (fresh water, salt water, oil) on the result

**Not good for:** core-calibrated porosity or permeability, grain-density lab measurements, or capillary-pressure data, carbonate, evaporite or coal reservoir characterization beyond the density lookup tables, seismic inversion, AVO, or the time-lapse/seismic work Blackfoot is more commonly cited for, depositional-facies or sequence-stratigraphic interpretation of the Glauconitic channel system, multi-mineral solver or NMR/ELAN-style modern probabilistic petrophysics, any basin outside the Western Canada Sedimentary Basin, or wells lacking a compensated density log

**Scope:** Western Canada Sedimentary Basin, Lower Cretaceous (Mannville/Glauconitic) siliciclastics at Blackfoot, southern Alberta; three-well case study using conventional open-hole logs, so numbers are local and the method assumes clean-to-shaly sandstone with a simple two-component matrix. Section labelling in this corpus is unreliable (a 'conglomerate petrophysics' heading fronts general density-log theory), so confirm section content before citing.
