# drillopedia-log-interpretation

**Covers:** A practical how-to reference on reading and interpreting open-hole wireline logs — tool responses (GR, resistivity, RHOB, neutron, sonic), curve combinations, typical log signatures by lithology, and rule-of-thumb reservoir property estimates.

**Authoritative for:** what a given curve physically responds to and how to read it (GR, deep/shallow resistivity, bulk density, neutron porosity), common cutoffs and rules of thumb: GR clean-sand cutoffs, resistivity thresholds hinting hydrocarbons vs water, calculating density porosity from RHOB with matrix and fluid density assumptions, recognizing neutron-density crossover and gas effect on logs, characteristic log signatures for clean sandstone, shale, and carbonate reservoirs, distinguishing shale from reservoir on GR plus resistivity, first-pass permeability estimates from conventional curve suites, field-level QC and interpretation workflow habits

**Not good for:** rigorous petrophysical evaluation requiring Archie/Waxman-Smits parameter derivation, core calibration, or uncertainty analysis, tool physics, calibration, and engineering detail at service-company manual depth, NMR, dielectric, borehole image, formation-test, or advanced/production logging interpretation, mud-log, LWD/geosteering, or cased-hole log interpretation, basin- or field-specific cutoffs, m/n exponents, or Rw values — the numbers here are generic, seismic, structural, stratigraphic, or basin-modeling questions, volumetrics, reserves booking, or completion design decisions, source-rock and unconventional log evaluation (TOC, brittleness, kerogen corrections)

**Scope:** Modern conventional open-hole logging practice, presented as a generic global primer with no basin, operator, or era anchoring. Cutoffs and thresholds are illustrative rules of thumb, not calibrated parameters — treat them as starting points to be replaced by local core and formation-water data. Encyclopedia-style secondary source: no primary data, no case-study wells, no citations, so it should not be cited as evidence for a specific field's properties.
