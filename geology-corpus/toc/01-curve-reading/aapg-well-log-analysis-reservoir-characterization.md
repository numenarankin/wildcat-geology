# aapg-well-log-analysis-reservoir-characterization

**Covers:** A practical well-log analysis and reservoir-characterization primer: curve-by-curve reading plus the standard petrophysical and rock-physics equations, illustrated with the South Barrow 18 well (North Slope, Alaska).

**Authoritative for:** which log curve to use for what (GR, SP, CALI, LLS/LLD, RHOB, NPHI, DT, PEF) and the order of an interpretation workflow, lithology calls from GR/SP/resistivity/density — sandstone vs shaly sand vs sandy shale vs shale, GR API ranges, shale volume from gamma ray via the Larionov equation and GR index, density and neutron-density porosity, including matrix densities for sandstone, limestone, dolomite, anhydrite, salt, and gas-effect handling, water saturation with the Simandoux (shaly-sand) equation, and choice of a and m for sand/carbonate/shaly sand, estimating Rw from SP, Rmf and bottom-hole temperature (Rwe route), Coates permeability estimation from porosity and irreducible water saturation, elastic properties from logs: acoustic and shear impedance, Castagna Vp/Vs from sonic porosity and Vclay, Poisson's ratio, reflectivity coefficient and its limits for hydrocarbon (bright/dim spot) inference, recognizing washouts/bad hole on caliper vs bit size and knowing where not to interpret

**Not good for:** core-calibrated or basin-specific parameter sets (a, m, n, Rw) for other fields — the constants here are generic defaults, modern probabilistic/multimineral inversion, NMR, dielectric, image-log or LWD-specific interpretation, carbonate-specific porosity partitioning, fracture characterization, or secondary-porosity workflows, seismic inversion, AVO modelling, or wavelet/tuning effects beyond a single-interface reflectivity coefficient, borehole geophysics theory, tool physics, environmental corrections and log editing procedures, volumetrics, reserves estimation, or completion/production engineering decisions, formation-evaluation practice in unconventional or high-clay-conductivity shale plays

**Scope:** Textbook/tutorial-level clastic-focused workflow using classic open-hole triple-combo logs; equations are the mid-to-late 20th-century empirical standards (Archie/Simandoux, Larionov, Coates, Castagna, Wyllie-type relations). Worked example is a single Arctic Alaska (South Barrow) gas well, so numeric cutoffs, GR ranges and matrix assumptions must be re-calibrated before use in another basin. Assumes water-based mud and reasonable hole conditions.
