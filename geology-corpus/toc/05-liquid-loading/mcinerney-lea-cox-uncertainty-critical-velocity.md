# mcinerney-lea-cox-uncertainty-critical-velocity

**Covers:** A technical paper (McInerney, Lea, Cox) on uncertainty in gas-well critical velocity predictions for liquid loading, comparing Turner, Coleman, and Shell/Kees correlations with derivations and measured data appendices.

**Authoritative for:** Turner droplet-model critical velocity derivation from drag/gravity balance, rationale for Turner's 20% upward adjustment and Coleman's unadjusted form, choice of drag coefficient and the Weber number of 30 in droplet breakup, Turner critical velocity equations for water vs condensate, Shell/Kees critical velocity correlation form, uncertainty and low-wellhead-pressure over-prediction behavior of Turner, comparison against Texas Tech measured loading data

**Not good for:** multiphase flow correlations for oil wells or general vertical lift performance, artificial lift equipment selection, sizing, or economics, deviated/horizontal well liquid holdup and slug flow modeling, reservoir characterization, decline analysis, or reserves estimation, field-specific operating recommendations outside gas wells, surfactant, plunger lift, or velocity string design details

**Scope:** Vertical (near-vertical) gas wells producing water and/or condensate; droplet-transport (Turner-family) critical velocity framework only, evaluated largely against Turner-era and Texas Tech laboratory/field datasets. Not basin-specific, but assumptions on drag coefficient, droplet size, and tubing geometry limit applicability to low-pressure, high-liquid-rate, or strongly deviated wells.
