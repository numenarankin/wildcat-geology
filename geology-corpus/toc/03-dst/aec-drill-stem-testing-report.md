# aec-drill-stem-testing-report

**Covers:** An operational primer on drill-stem testing (DST): tool-string components, test design and gauge/valve/jar placement, cushions, and segment-by-segment interpretation of the DST pressure chart.

**Authoritative for:** what a DST measures and the four basic components of a DST tool assembly (packer, test valve, by-pass/equalizing valve, pressure recorder), function and placement of test valve, circulating valve, hydraulic by-pass, jars, and safety joints in the string, how many subsurface pressure gauges to run and where to place them; gauge pressure-rating selection, use of water or gas cushions in DST design, reading a DST pressure chart point-by-point (segments A-B through G-H etc.), identifying static reservoir pressure and initial/final shut-in periods, rule-of-thumb durations for initial shut-in periods

**Not good for:** quantitative pressure-transient analysis math (Horner plots, superposition, skin and permeability calculations, radius of investigation), modern electronic memory gauges, downhole real-time telemetry, or current tubing-conveyed/closed-chamber test practice, wireline formation testers, MDT/RFT-style pressure sampling, or production well testing, fluid sampling, PVT analysis, or hydrocarbon composition interpretation, log analysis, petrophysical saturation or porosity calculations, field-, basin-, or formation-specific reservoir pressures, gradients, or productivity data, well control, HSE regulation, or blowout-prevention requirements as currently enforced

**Scope:** A generic, era-limited engineering handbook/report treatment of conventional mechanical drill-stem tools and clock-driven pressure charts; procedures and gauge technology reflect older practice and are not tied to any specific basin or operator, so tool specifics and shut-in-time guidance should be checked against current service-company and regulatory standards before field use.
