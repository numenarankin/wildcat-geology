# aapg-drill-stem-testing

**Covers:** A practical AAPG-style methods chapter on drill stem testing (DST): job planning and tool/packer selection, chart and pressure-record interpretation, fluid-recovery measurement, and quantitative buildup analysis (wellbore storage, Horner plots, transmissibility, skin, radius of investigation) with worked examples.

**Authoritative for:** recommended DST flow and shut-in period durations and the rationale for a final buildup ~3x the final flow period, packer seat selection and DST job planning considerations, reading and diagnosing DST pressure charts, including common interpretation pitfalls, measuring fluid recovery on nonflowing DSTs (drill pipe fluid column and gradient, why not to reverse out to a pit), surface tests on DST fluid samples: grindout, chloride, resistivity, estimating instantaneous and average flow rates from DST pressure data (superposition, Odeh-Selig, simplified Horner comparisons), calculating measured wellbore storage capacity/constant and recognizing unit-slope log-log storage effects, Horner plot construction, P* extrapolation for static reservoir pressure, slope selection using the first buildup, and interpreting lower second-buildup extrapolations, skin factor, transmissibility, and radius of investigation from DST buildups, gas-cut mud complications in verifying recovery

**Not good for:** modern permanent downhole gauge or production-logging pressure transient workflows, full modern well test analysis theory (type-curve matching, deconvolution, derivative analysis beyond storage diagnostics), cased-hole production testing, extended well tests, or perforated-interval completion design, multiphase/gas-condensate or unconventional (fractured horizontal) test interpretation, formation-tester (RFT/MDT) or wireline pressure data interpretation, log analysis, petrophysical saturation calculations, or reserve estimation, regional stratigraphy, basin-specific reservoir descriptions, or economics beyond the payout anecdote, specific vendor tool hardware specifications or current safety/regulatory requirements

**Scope:** Open-hole drill stem testing as practiced in conventional vertical exploration wells, oriented to hand/spreadsheet analytical methods (Horner semilog, radial flow, single-phase slightly compressible fluid assumptions) with mechanical-chart-era data. Nomenclature and unit conventions are oilfield/US customary; equations assume homogeneous radial reservoirs and short test durations, so results are radius-of-investigation limited. Treat as interpretation methodology, not as current operational or HSE guidance.
