# earlougher-well-test-analysis-part2-1977

**Covers:** The second half of Earlougher's SPE Monograph 5, *Advances in Well Test Analysis* (1977), covering drillstem and wireline formation tests, multiple-well interference and pulse testing, reservoir heterogeneities, wellbore/completion effects on transient tests, computer-aided analysis, and test design and instrumentation (Chapters 8-13).

**Authoritative for:** Drillstem test (DST) interpretation: valve sequence, chart reading, Horner buildup analysis, producing-time choice, damage ratio, flow-period type-curve (Ramey-Agarwal-Martin) matching, and troubleshooting mechanically bad charts, Interference test analysis by exponential-integral type-curve matching and long-time semilog methods, including phi*ct estimation and bounded-system curves, Kamal-Brigham pulse-test design and analysis (time lag, response amplitude, cycle time) for both horizontal well-to-well and vertical (Falade-Brigham) pulse tests, Determining permeability anisotropy direction and magnitude from multi-observation-well interference tests (Ramey/Papadopulos method), Diagnosing heterogeneities from semilog slope changes: sealing faults and distance-to-fault, composite radial systems, layered reservoirs with and without crossflow, naturally fractured dual-porosity two-parallel-line behavior, Vertical permeability determination from vertical interference and vertical pulse tests, including boundary-affected iterative analysis, Wellbore storage coefficient calculation (liquid-filled, falling liquid level, gas column), changing storage diagnosis, and storage end-time criteria, Vertically and horizontally fractured well analysis: square-root-of-time plots, fracture half-length xf, half-slope log-log diagnosis, pseudoradial flow, Partial penetration and slanted-well pseudoskin, Pressure transient test design: test-type selection, shut-in duration, gauge sensitivity and range, data checklists, mechanical pressure gauges

**Not good for:** Basic single-well drawdown and buildup theory, Horner/MDH/MBH fundamentals, and average reservoir pressure methods (these are in Part 1 of the same monograph), Modern pressure-derivative and deconvolution diagnostics, which postdate the book, Modern high-resolution electronic gauges, permanent downhole monitoring, or memory-gauge DST practice; instrumentation discussion is 1970s mechanical Bourdon-tube/Amerada era, Modern finite-conductivity fracture and multi-fractured horizontal well models; fracture treatment is uniform-flux and infinite-conductivity vertical/horizontal fractures only, Horizontal and multilateral well testing, Unconventional shale/tight-oil rate-transient analysis, though a section on pressure-dependent rock properties in low-permeability gas sands is included, Wireline formation tester interpretation in depth (only a short introductory section), Numerical simulation practice, gridding, or history-matching workflows beyond a conceptual superposition-based discussion, Field-specific or basin-specific reservoir descriptions; examples (e.g. Arbuckle DST) are illustrative, not regional studies

**Scope:** US petroleum engineering practice as of 1977, oilfield units throughout; single-phase slightly compressible liquid flow assumed unless otherwise noted, with gas handled via pseudopressure approximations. Type-curve and chart-based hand methods dominate, relying on the book's own figure and appendix tables (Figs. C.x etc.), so worked examples cannot be reproduced without the plates. Still the standard reference for the analytical basis of DST, interference, and pulse testing, but pre-dates derivative analysis, and its instrumentation, computing, and fracture-model content is dated.
