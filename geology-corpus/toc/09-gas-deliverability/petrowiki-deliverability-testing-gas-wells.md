# petrowiki-deliverability-testing-gas-wells

**Covers:** A PetroWiki reference chapter on deliverability testing of gas wells: test types (flow-after-flow, single-point, isochronal, modified isochronal), the theoretical Houpeurt and empirical Rawlins-Schellhardt backpressure equations, stabilization-time/radius-of-investigation estimation, and nomenclature with source citations.

**Authoritative for:** defining absolute open flow (AOF) potential and how it is estimated from deliverability tests, choosing among flow-after-flow, single-point, isochronal, and modified isochronal test designs, Houpeurt transient and pseudosteady-state gas deliverability equations and their pseudopressure vs pressure-squared forms, interpreting the a and b coefficients of the quadratic (Houpeurt) deliverability equation and the non-Darcy flow constant D, estimating stabilization time and radius of investigation, and judging when pseudosteady state begins for various drainage-area shapes, standard symbols, units, and classic literature citations for gas backpressure and real-gas flow theory

**Not good for:** oil-well or multiphase (liquid-loading, condensate banking) deliverability analysis, pressure-transient interpretation for permeability/skin from buildup or drawdown tests beyond what the deliverability equations require, field-specific or basin-specific production performance data and case studies, nodal analysis, tubing/wellbore hydraulics, surface facility or gathering-system constraints, hydraulic-fracture or horizontal-well specific deliverability models and unconventional/shale gas rate-transient analysis, reservoir characterization, stratigraphy, or any geological description

**Scope:** Generic petroleum-engineering encyclopedia material: single-phase dry-gas flow in vertical wells, radial homogeneous reservoirs, and oilfield units. Equations assume stabilized or transient Darcy plus non-Darcy flow and are location-independent, so no basin, formation, or regulatory-jurisdiction specificity; test-design guidance reflects conventional-gas practice from the classic (1930s-1990s) literature rather than modern unconventional workflows.
