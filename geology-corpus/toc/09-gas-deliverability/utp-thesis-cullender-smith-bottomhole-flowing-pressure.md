# utp-thesis-cullender-smith-bottomhole-flowing-pressure

**Covers:** An undergraduate/graduate thesis (Universiti Teknologi PETRONAS) presenting an Excel/VBA implementation of the Cullender & Smith and Average Temperature & Deviation Factor methods for computing gas well bottom-hole flowing pressure from surface data, with worked examples, VBA code, and validation against measured pressures.

**Authoritative for:** step-by-step Cullender & Smith calculation procedure, including midpoint-pressure iteration and Simpson's rule refinement, Average Temperature and Deviation Factor method equations (S term, Pwf) and its assumptions, Excel/VBA formula implementations, GoalSeek usage, and complete VBA code listings for both methods, friction factor and Reynolds number terms for a vertical gas flow string, pseudocritical/pseudoreduced property and z-factor correlations as coded in a spreadsheet, reported percent-error comparisons: Cullender & Smith vs Average T&z vs measured pressure, and VBA-for-Excel vs VB.NET numerical differences, symbol definitions and variable conventions for gas well pressure-traverse equations

**Not good for:** multiphase or liquid-loaded gas wells, oil wells, and gas-lift or two-phase flow correlations (Hagedorn-Brown, Beggs-Brill, etc.), horizontal or highly deviated wellbore pressure traverse, reservoir inflow performance, deliverability testing, or well test interpretation beyond flowing BHP, field-wide or basin-specific production data; validation rests on a very small well dataset, modern commercial nodal-analysis workflows, temperature modeling, or transient wellbore simulation, authoritative z-factor or PVT correlation review; correlations are used, not evaluated

**Scope:** Student thesis, single-phase dry gas vertical wells, steady-state flow; validated on a handful of wells only, so quoted percent errors are illustrative rather than statistically general. Methods themselves are classic (Cullender & Smith 1956) and era-independent, but the software discussion (VBA for Excel vs VB.NET) is dated implementation detail. No named basin or field of general interest; treat well data as anonymized examples.
