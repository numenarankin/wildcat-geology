# ijmret-bottomhole-pressure-gradient-methods-comparison

**Covers:** A comparative study (IJMRET journal paper) evaluating three analytical methods — Average T and Z, Sukkar and Cornell, and Cullender and Smith — for calculating static and flowing bottom-hole pressure in gas wells, benchmarked against Niger Delta field data and applied to gas deliverability/AOF estimation.

**Authoritative for:** Governing equations and step-by-step workflow for the Average T and Z, Sukkar and Cornell, and Cullender and Smith bottom-hole pressure methods, Method selection guidance for static vs flowing BHP in vertical versus deviated gas wells, Expected error magnitudes and depth/pressure validity limits of each BHP correlation as tested on Niger Delta gas wells, Converting wellhead pressure measurements into flowing bottom-hole pressure when no downhole gauge is run, Incorporating water or condensate production into flowing BHP calculations, Definition and determination of absolute open flow (AOF) and gas well deliverability from BHP estimates

**Not good for:** Oil-well or multiphase-dominated pressure traverse calculations, and mechanistic/empirical multiphase flow correlations (Hagedorn-Brown, Beggs-Brill, etc.), Pressure transient/well test interpretation, reservoir characterization, or reserves estimation, Regional geology, stratigraphy, or depositional history of the Niger Delta, Modern numerical wellbore/nodal simulation, coupled thermal modelling, or ESP/artificial lift design, Basin-specific fluid property correlations outside the tested gas composition and PVT range

**Scope:** Petroleum production engineering, not geology; single-phase (dry to lightly wet) gas wells only. Calibration and error statistics derive from a limited set of Niger Delta gas wells, so quoted accuracies and the Sukkar-Cornell depth/pressure validity window should not be assumed transferable to other basins, very deep/HPHT wells, or high-liquid-loading wells. Methods are classical analytical correlations (1950s-60s origin) presented in a modest-tier journal; treat quantitative error rankings as indicative rather than definitive.
