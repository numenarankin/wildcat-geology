# onepetro-water-and-gas-coning

**Covers:** A petroleum-engineering handbook chapter on water and gas coning in producing wells: definitions, critical-rate prediction methods (Chaperon, Schols, Chierici et al.), partial-penetration flow, and coning-control strategies, with worked examples.

**Authoritative for:** definitions and distinctions between coning, cusping, free gas-cap production, and rising water/oil-contact water production, critical (coning-free) production rate concepts and what a critical-rate calculation does and does not predict, Chaperon critical-rate calculation for vertical and horizontal wells, including kv/kh anisotropy effects, Schols empirical critical-rate correlation and effect of perforation length, Chierici et al. dimensionless chart correlation (psi, epsilon, delta, rDe) for water, gas, or simultaneous gas-and-water coning, selecting optimum perforation length and standoff position to delay coning, partially penetrating well productivity (Kozeny-type equation), comparing horizontal vs vertical well coning tendency in a given drainage/spacing unit, practical coning mitigation and rate-management guidance

**Not good for:** predicting time to water/gas breakthrough or post-breakthrough WOR/GOR behavior, full-field or numerical reservoir simulation workflows and gridding of near-well cones, waterflood or gas-injection sweep design, displacement efficiency, or fractional-flow analysis, water shutoff hardware, gels, cement squeezes, or ICD/AICD completion design details, log or production-log diagnosis of water entry in a specific well, multiphase wellbore hydraulics, artificial lift, or surface handling of produced water/gas, fractured, dual-porosity, or highly heterogeneous/layered reservoirs where analytical cone theory breaks down, unconventional/tight reservoirs and hydraulically fractured horizontal wells, economic optimization of rate vs recovery

**Scope:** Classic analytical/empirical coning theory for conventional oil reservoirs with a gas cap and/or bottomwater, assuming homogeneous, anisotropic single-layer formations and steady-state flow; handbook-style presentation with US oilfield units and 160-acre spacing examples; correlations date from roughly the 1970s-1990s and are screening tools, not substitutes for simulation.
