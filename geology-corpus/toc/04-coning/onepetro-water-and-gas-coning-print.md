# onepetro-water-and-gas-coning-print

**Covers:** A petroleum-engineering handbook chapter on water and gas coning in oil wells, covering coning mechanics, critical-rate prediction methods (Chaperon, Schols, Chierici et al.), perforation-interval optimization, and partially penetrating well rate estimation, with worked numerical examples.

**Authoritative for:** defining water coning vs. rising water/oil contact, and gas coning vs. free gas-cap production, calculating critical (cone-stable) production rate for vertical wells using Chaperon, Schols, and Chierici et al. correlations, critical-rate calculation for horizontal wells, including the aHD dimensionless parameter and F factor, effect of kv/kh anisotropy, perforation length (b/h), and perforation position on coning tendency, selecting optimum perforation length and standoff to avoid simultaneous gas and water coning, using the Chierici et al. dimensionless charts (delta_g, delta_w, epsilon, r_De), estimating producing rate of a partially penetrating well (Kozeny-type relation), worked example calculations comparing correlation results and reconciling their differences

**Not good for:** predicting time to water/gas breakthrough or post-breakthrough WOR/GOR behavior — critical rate theory does not address these, numerical reservoir simulation of coning, or gridding/near-well modeling guidance, remediation after breakthrough (gels, shutoff, ICDs, downhole water sinks) beyond general control strategy comments, field- or basin-specific coning case histories or empirical field data, reservoir characterization, log analysis, sedimentology, or stratigraphy, gas well liquid loading, condensate banking, or non-coning inflow problems, unconventional or fractured-reservoir inflow behavior

**Scope:** Classic analytical/empirical petroleum production engineering, homogeneous or simply layered reservoirs with a mobile bottom water or gas cap; correlations are steady-state, single-well, and assume idealized geometry and known kv/kh. Oilfield units and chart-based lookups. Generic — not tied to any basin or region; results between correlations disagree by design and should be treated as bounding estimates rather than predictions.
